# Projection Principles

## Purpose

This document defines the principles governing projections of the architectural framework into different media.

Projections include, but are not limited to:

- novel
- libretto
- film
- essay
- research fragments


---

# Structural Independence

Each projection constitutes an independent realization of the architecture.

Projections do not adapt one another.

They derive from the same structural foundations while remaining formally autonomous.


---

# Projection Discipline

Every projection introduces reduction.

Each work should therefore clarify:

- which aspects of the architecture are emphasized
- which aspects remain outside the frame
- which distortions arise from the chosen medium


---

# Structural Fidelity

A projection remains valid only if it preserves the core structural dynamics:

- epistemic movement
- structural tension
- recurrence patterns
- institutional environments


---

# Medium Logic

Each medium possesses its own formal logic.

Examples:

- narrative articulation in the novel
- musical and vocal structure in the libretto
- spatial and visual dynamics in film

A projection must remain faithful to the logic of its medium.


---

# Non-Coercion

Structural correspondences between projections may emerge naturally.

They must never be enforced.

Forced alignment weakens both the medium and the architecture.


---

# Summary

Projections are independent realizations of a shared architecture.

Their differences are not deviations but expressions of the reductions inherent in each medium.
