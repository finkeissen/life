# Core

The **core** holds the stable architecture of `life`.

It defines:

- structural dimensions
- irreducible limits and simplifications
- invariants that persist across projects, media, tone, and interpretation
- the shared vocabulary through which projections remain mutually legible

The core does **not** provide narratives, characters, settings, or solutions.
It is not a theory of progress, redemption, or meaning completion.

The core specifies what cannot be avoided
when engaging human life under conditions of finitude, opacity, constraint, and misalignment.

---

## Function

The core exists to:

- stabilize the repository against conceptual drift
- prevent implicit teleology
- prevent hidden moralization
- provide a shared structural reference for all projections
- preserve compatibility across project-level reductions

It answers structural questions only, such as:

- What kinds of limits are unavoidable?
- Which tensions recur across contexts?
- Where does responsibility arise?
- Which distortions remain admissible without collapsing the architecture?

The core does not decide how these questions must be narrated, staged, or embodied.

---

## Files

- `architecture.md`  
  The structural dimensions and layers of `life`.

- `principles.md`  
  Invariants governing projection, responsibility, loss, and constraint.

- `glossary.md`  
  Shared terms used across the repository.

These files should be read together.
No single file exhausts the core.

---

## Scope and Abstraction Level

The core operates at a structural level of abstraction.

It does not describe:

- individual biographies
- historical contingencies
- psychological profiles
- cultural specifics
- medium-dependent dramaturgies
- project-specific figures or symbolic carriers

These belong to projections.

The core remains intentionally underdetermined with respect to content,
while being overdetermined with respect to constraints.

It says little about who, where, and when.
It says more about what cannot be escaped.

---

## Relation to Projects

Projects derive from the core.

They do not found it,
verify it,
complete it,
or supersede it.

A project may:

- intensify a core aspect
- expose a tension
- reveal a blind spot
- fail to carry the structure
- restate selected assumptions locally for readability

Such outcomes do **not** by themselves modify the core.

Only repeated and structurally consistent pressure
from across projections
may justify core revision.

The core therefore permits project-level overlap,
but not project-level redefinition.

---

## Misalignment as Organizing Concept

The core assumes persistent **misalignment**
between:

- perception
- self-understanding
- action
- consequence
- reality

This misalignment is:

- structural
- non-pathological
- irreducible

Outcomes emerging from misalignment
may include learning, adaptation, conflict, stagnation, repetition, or failure.

No outcome is privileged as final or normative.

The architecture does not assume that contradiction culminates in resolution.

---

## Development Without Teleology

The core acknowledges **development**
as change in relative adequacy
between capacity and constraint.

Development:

- has no predefined direction
- has no guaranteed elevation
- has no final state
- does not imply moral improvement

Regression, stagnation, repetition, distortion, and local gain with global loss
are all structurally admissible trajectories.

The core therefore rejects progress as an invariant.

---

## Responsibility Under Constraint

Responsibility arises
where bounded agents act under conditions
they do not fully control, comprehend, or stabilize.

Responsibility:

- precedes full understanding
- survives failure
- cannot be dissolved by explanation
- cannot be discharged by resonance alone
- remains structurally binding under uncertainty

The core records this as a condition,
not as a moral instruction
and not as a promise of adequacy.

---

## Stability and Change Policy

The core should change rarely.

Permissible reasons for change include:

- clarification of structure
- explicit resolution of contradiction
- integration of a genuinely missing invariant
- removal of terminology that produces systematic confusion

Impermissible reasons include:

- adaptation to a specific project
- accommodation to a preferred medium
- aesthetic preference
- motivational rhetoric
- explanatory expansion beyond structural necessity

A stable core is not a rigid core.
But changes must be architecture-driven, not projection-driven.

---

## Use as Architectural Reference

The core is intended to be:

- cited by projects
- restated in part by project READMEs where needed
- stable across time
- readable without knowledge of any specific work
- minimal in claim, strong in constraint

It is not intended to be:

- exhaustive
- persuasive
- inspirational
- genre-bound
- dependent on any single realization

The core should remain intelligible
even if all current projects were removed.

---

## Incompleteness

The core is complete as architecture,
not as representation.

What it leaves open
is not merely a gap,
but the necessary space in which projection becomes possible.

Silence at the core level
is therefore not failure.
It is structural restraint.

The core must leave room
for project-level concentration,
distortion, emphasis, and loss
without losing its own identity.
