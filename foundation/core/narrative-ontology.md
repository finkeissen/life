# Narrative Ontology

## Purpose

This document defines the ontological rules governing narrative events in BEING.
It specifies what constitutes an event, how responsibility emerges, and why
structural recurrence replaces traditional narrative resolution.

The ontology applies across all projections (novel, opera, film, essay,
installation).

---

## Core Principle

Narrative in BEING is not driven by conflict resolution but by the emergence of
non-delegable responsibility.

Events do not resolve problems.
They expose the impossibility of escaping participation in structural reality.

---

## Event Definition

An event occurs when a situation reveals that responsibility cannot be
delegated.

Events are not defined by external action but by epistemic shift.

An event must therefore satisfy at least one of the following conditions:

- A previously stable interpretation becomes untenable.
- A decision becomes irreversible.
- Responsibility becomes non-transferable.
- Moral relief becomes unavailable.

---

## Structural Recurrence

Narrative structures in BEING recur rather than resolve.

Recurrence does not mean repetition of plot but reappearance of structural
conditions:

- responsibility without innocence
- participation without control
- continuation without redemption

Each recurrence deepens recognition without providing closure.

---

## Responsibility

Responsibility emerges when the protagonist recognizes that:

1. no external authority can absorb the consequence of action
2. structural participation cannot be revoked
3. moral innocence cannot be restored

Responsibility is therefore not assigned but uncovered.

---

## Characters

Characters function as structural carriers rather than psychological subjects.

Their primary narrative role is to reveal different modes of participation in
the same structural conditions.

Biographical details may appear but do not define the character’s ontological
function.

---

## Identity Ambiguity

Across projections, the identity of Ekkehard may appear as:

- a single recurring person
- structurally resonant individuals
- a narrative projection of responsibility

The project deliberately refuses to resolve this ambiguity.

The ambiguity itself is a structural feature.

---

## Narrative Outcome

Narratives in BEING do not culminate in:

- redemption
- punishment
- moral clarification
- restoration of innocence

Instead, narratives end with **open structural continuation**.

The final state is not resolution but recognition.
