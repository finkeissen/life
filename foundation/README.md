# Foundation

`foundation/` contains the structural basis of the `life` repository.

This layer gathers the materials that define, stabilize, clarify, test,
and maintain the architecture beneath any single project realization.

It is not itself a project space.
It does not produce narrative realization.
It does not depend on any one medium or work.

Its task is to hold the conditions
under which projects can be developed without conceptual drift.

---

## Function of the Foundation Layer

The foundation layer exists to:

- define structural assumptions
- preserve shared invariants
- articulate models and relations
- record governance for projection discipline
- maintain meta-level orientation across the repository
- distinguish stable architecture from active projection work

`foundation/` therefore carries the repository
at the level of structure, vocabulary, method, and constraint.

It is broader than the core,
but still prior to projects.

---

## Relation to the Rest of the Repository

The repository is not divided simply into theory and application.

Instead:

- `foundation/` holds the structural basis
- `projects/` holds selective projections and realizations
- the root level later names the repository as a whole

The foundation layer may contain different abstraction levels,
but these remain subordinate to one task:
to stabilize the architectural field
within which projections become possible.

Projects may restate selected assumptions locally.
They do not replace the foundation.

The foundation may orient projects.
It does not itself become one.

---

## Internal Structure of `foundation/`

The folder currently includes several distinct but related sublayers.

### `core/`

The most stable architectural layer.

This is where the repository defines
its primary structural vocabulary,
its deepest constraints,
and the invariants that should remain legible
across projects, media, and interpretations.

`core/` should remain the least project-dependent part of the repository.

### `invariants/`

The layer in which recurring structural conditions
are stated more explicitly and in greater thematic spread.

If `core/` holds the densest architectural nucleus,
`invariants/` unfolds the conditions
that should remain binding across projections.

### `models/`

Operational and comparative structures
used to make relations, tensions, and dynamics more thinkable.

Models are not fiction,
but neither are they equivalent to the core itself.
They organize structural patterns
without exhausting architecture.

### `governance/`

Repository discipline.

This layer records rules, protocols, and normative constraints
for how projections should be formed,
declared, and maintained.

Governance does not replace architecture,
but it regulates how architectural work is carried into practice.

### `meta/`

Orientation, comparison, and reflective support.

This layer may include summaries,
gap analyses,
historical comparisons,
warnings against distortion,
and higher-order reflections on the state of the repository.

It is interpretive and reflective,
but should not silently become a second core.

### `docs/`

Process and editorial guidance.

This layer contains working documents
that help organize revision, sequencing, and maintenance,
without introducing independent architectural claims.

---

## Layer Logic Within the Foundation

These sublayers are related,
but not identical.

A useful working distinction is:

- **core** defines the deepest architecture
- **invariants** state recurring constraints more broadly
- **models** organize dynamic relations
- **governance** regulates repository discipline
- **meta** reflects on structure and incompleteness
- **docs** records editorial and process logic

Some overlap is unavoidable
and in parts desirable.

But overlap should remain legible.
The question is not whether two layers touch,
but whether they still differ in function.

---

## On Overlap Inside the Foundation

This repository does not treat overlap as automatically defective.

A concept may appear in more than one sublayer
if its function changes with the layer:

- in `core/` as invariant
- in `models/` as relation
- in `meta/` as reflection
- in `governance/` as discipline
- in `docs/` as editorial guidance

What must be avoided is not repetition,
but unnoticed change of status.

The rule is therefore:

**repetition across layers is acceptable; status confusion is not.**

---

## What the Foundation Layer Must Not Do

Even taken as a whole, `foundation/` should not:

- collapse into project language
- derive its structure from one favored realization
- silently absorb temporary process decisions into architecture
- confuse reflective commentary with invariant structure
- become dependent on a single medium, genre, or protagonist

The more complete the foundation becomes,
the more important this restraint is.

A broad foundation is not an unrestricted one.

---

## Use by Projects

Projects depend on the foundation unevenly.

Some may lean more heavily on:

- `core/` for vocabulary and invariants
- `invariants/` for explicit structural conditions
- `models/` for patterning and relation
- `governance/` for declared projection discipline
- `meta/` for comparative orientation

Not every project must cite every sublayer directly.

But no project should proceed
as if the foundation were optional.

---

## Incompleteness and Growth

The foundation layer may grow over time.

New files or subfolders are justified
when they clarify a genuinely distinct function,
not when they merely rename existing material.

Growth is legitimate
if it increases structural legibility.

Growth becomes distortion
when it multiplies layers
without sharpening their role.

The test is simple:

Does a new element carry a distinct task
within the foundation,
or only duplicate an old one without added precision?

---

## Reminder

`foundation/` is the support architecture of the repository.

It is not the whole repository.
It is not a project.
It is not merely documentation.

It is the structured field
from which disciplined projections can emerge
without losing architectural coherence.
