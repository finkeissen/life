# Formal Architecture

## Purpose

This document defines the structural framework through which the narrative
ontology of BEING is expressed in concrete narrative form.

It describes the progression of epistemic states rather than traditional plot
development.

---

## Structural Progression

The narrative moves through a sequence of epistemic zones.

Each zone represents a different relationship between the protagonist and
structural responsibility.

The zones are not strictly chronological but form a directional progression.

---

## Zone A — Supported Self-Evidence

The protagonist operates within a stable interpretive framework.

Characteristics:

- trust in institutional or moral structures
- belief in delegable responsibility
- confidence in causal clarity

Narrative function:
Establish the conditions that later become untenable.

---

## Zone B — Inner Irritation

Small anomalies appear within the existing interpretive structure.

Characteristics:

- minor contradictions
- discomfort with inherited explanations
- first doubts about delegation of responsibility

Narrative function:
Introduce epistemic instability without immediate collapse.

---

## Zone C — Non-Delegability

The protagonist confronts situations where responsibility cannot be transferred.

Characteristics:

- decisions with irreversible consequences
- institutional protection fails
- personal involvement becomes unavoidable

Narrative function:
Shift the narrative center from observation to participation.

---

## Zone D — Loss of Moral Relief

Traditional mechanisms of moral stabilization cease to function.

Characteristics:

- absence of clear guilt distribution
- recognition that innocence is impossible
- collapse of redemption narratives

Narrative function:
Remove the possibility of narrative consolation.

---

## Zone E — Open Continuation

The protagonist recognizes structural participation as permanent.

Characteristics:

- awareness without resolution
- responsibility without absolution
- continuation without narrative closure

Narrative function:
Conclude the narrative without restoring stability.

---

## Structural Mirrors

Events may recur across different projections or narrative moments.

Mirrors are not repetitions of plot but structural echoes:

- similar decisions under different circumstances
- repeated recognition of non-delegability
- parallel moments across media

Mirrors reinforce the recurrence principle of the project.

---

## Projection Discipline

Each medium emphasizes different aspects of the architecture.

Examples:

- Opera intensifies emotional recognition.
- Film reveals situational pressure.
- Essay clarifies conceptual implications.
- Installation spatializes structural recurrence.

No projection may introduce elements that contradict the narrative ontology.

---

## Formal Invariants

Across all projections the following rules apply:

- no heroic resolution
- no moral purification
- no ultimate explanation
- no restoration of innocence

These invariants preserve the structural identity of the project.
