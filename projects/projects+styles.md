# Projects + Styles

This document defines how the `life` repository can generate
multiple **projects**
and, within each project,
multiple **styles**
without dissolving the common structural frame.

It protects two things at once:

- structural coherence across the repository
- formal openness across projections

A project or a style is **not required**
to represent the full architecture.

Partiality is not a defect.
It is a condition of projection.

What matters is not completeness,
but whether a projection can be
**located within the whole**
without falsely claiming to be the whole.

---

## Function of this document

This document introduces
a reusable projection logic:

- one shared structural frame
- many projects
- many styles within projects
- partial coverage as a valid mode
- explicit locatability within the total architecture
- generative reuse from minimal prompting

It therefore adds a layer between:

- the architectural ground of `life`
- concrete artistic or conceptual realizations

Its long-term purpose is not only classification,
but generation.

The repository should eventually support
the creation of new works
from very small inputs,
because project and style are meant to function
as reusable structural operators.

---

## Core distinction

### Project

A **project** is a bounded projection domain.

A project defines:

- a medium or form of realization
- a structural focus
- a typical scale of articulation
- a mode of reduction
- a characteristic strength
- a characteristic blindness

Examples:

- novel
- opera
- film
- essay
- installation
- notebook
- dramatic fragment
- voice study
- image cycle

A project is not defined by topic alone.
It is defined by the way
the architecture becomes legible through it.

---

### Style

A **style** is a secondary reduction within a project.

A style defines
**how** a given project articulates its material.

A style may shape:

- tone
- density
- rhythm
- degree of explicitness
- symbolic vs analytic pressure
- narrative distance
- degree of fragmentation
- sensory vs conceptual emphasis

Examples:

Within a novel project, styles might include:

- austere
- lyrical
- documentary
- polyphonic
- fragmentary
- absurd
- romantic
- mythically condensed

Within an opera project, styles might include:

- ritual
- chamber-minimal
- monumental
- ruptured
- liturgical
- dissonant-analytic

A style does not create a new architecture.
It modulates access to one bounded projection.

---

## Structural principle

The repository has one architecture,
but many valid cuts.

Therefore:

- not every project must cover every invariant
- not every style must activate every project dimension
- absence is permitted
- asymmetry is expected
- selective visibility is structurally normal

A project or style fails
not when it is partial,
but when it becomes
**structurally unlocatable**.

---

## Productive tension and apparent contradiction

Because each project and each style
is a partial projection,
different realizations may produce
statements that appear to contradict one another.

This is expected.

It does **not** mean
that the architecture is incoherent.

It means that life cannot be exhausted
from a single angle.

Different projections may emphasize:

- agency vs limitation
- intimacy vs structure
- continuity vs rupture
- hope vs irreversibility
- devotion vs estrangement
- local meaning vs global opacity

These tensions may lead to
apparently opposing claims.

Such opposition is not necessarily an error.
It is often the mark of a living structure.

The repository should therefore allow
**productive contradiction**:
not arbitrary contradiction,
but tension generated by
different bounded accesses
to the same world.

---

## Why contradiction matters

Life does not unfold
as a flat consistency.

It unfolds through tension:

- between possibility and limit
- between self-image and actuality
- between desire and consequence
- between orientation and blindness
- between what can be integrated
  and what resists integration

This tension is part of the drama of life.

Projects and styles should therefore be able
to render different sides of the same structure
without forcing premature reconciliation.

A repository about life
must permit local incompatibilities
as long as they remain globally locatable.

---

## Consequence for personality development

These tensions are not merely descriptive.
They are generative.

When a projection reveals
a contradiction, asymmetry, or unresolved pressure,
this may indicate
a new developmental task.

In this repository,
personality development should not be treated
as a smooth growth narrative,
but as work emerging from tension.

That means:

- contradictions can reveal unfinished integration
- blind spots can indicate developmental demands
- stylistic or projective limits can point to missing capacities
- what cannot yet be held together
  may define the next task of formation

Therefore,
new tasks of personality development
may be derived from:

- repeated structural tension
- recurring blind spots
- mismatches between aspiration and form
- unresolved conflicts between projections

The repository should support this movement:
from structural tension
to developmental task.

---

## Requirement of locatability

Every project and every style
must be locatable within the overall frame.

Locatable means:

- its reduction can be named
- its focus can be named
- its exclusions can be named
- its relation to the core can be named
- its blind spots can be named
- its tensions with other projections can be named
- it does not present local emphasis as total truth

Locatability matters more than breadth.

A narrow projection can be valid.
A totalizing projection cannot.

## What a project must define

Each project should minimally specify:

### 1. Medium

Through which form does the projection appear?

Examples:
- prose
- staged voice
- moving image
- visual sequence
- reflective text
- score-like structure

### 2. Structural focus

Which part of the architecture becomes primary?

Examples:
- responsibility
- development
- local coherence under global blindness
- irreversibility
- institutional participation
- resonance without discharge

### 3. Projection strength

What does this project make especially visible?

### 4. Structural blind spot

What does this project tend to suppress,
flatten,
romanticize,
or over-organize?

### 5. Expected scale

At what scale does it operate best?

Examples:
- scene
- sequence
- whole life arc
- institutional episode
- reflective fragment
- ritualized event

### 6. Core linkage

Which invariants, models, tensions, or principles
does it primarily draw from?

### 7. Non-claim

What does this project explicitly not claim to cover?

This prevents overreach.

### 8. Developmental relevance

Which kinds of developmental task
does this project tend to make legible?

---

## What a style must define

Each style within a project should minimally specify:

### 1. Mode of articulation

How does the project speak?

Examples:
- sparse
- recursive
- high-symbolic
- observational
- cold
- intimate
- ceremonial
- fractured
- romantic
- absurd

### 2. Density profile

What is intensified?

Examples:
- atmosphere
- concept
- conflict
- silence
- repetition
- embodiment
- precision
- ambiguity

### 3. Relation to explanation

Does the style:

- explain
- imply
- withhold
- fracture explanation
- distribute explanation across voices

### 4. Relation to character or subject

Does the style privilege:

- inner access
- external behavior
- role logic
- collective voice
- symbolic function
- witness position

### 5. Formal pressure

What formal law dominates?

Examples:
- continuity
- montage
- recurrence
- contrast
- ritual return
- document layering
- scenic compression

### 6. Style-specific blind spot

Every style should state
what it tends to miss or distort.

### 7. Tension profile

Which kinds of contradiction,
friction,
or developmental pressure
does this style make visible especially well?

---

## Coverage is optional, accountability is not

A project or style may cover only a small subset
of the architectural field.

That is allowed.

But it must still remain accountable to the whole.

This means:

- it must not deny what it omits
- it must not redefine omission as irrelevance
- it must not turn stylistic preference into ontology
- it must remain compatible with the repository's invariants

A style can be narrow.
It cannot be self-absolute.

---

## Shared frame across heterogeneous projections

Projects and styles may differ radically in surface form
while still belonging to the same architecture.

Their unity does not come from
shared genre conventions,
shared plots,
or shared vocabulary.

Their unity comes from
shared structural submission to:

- world > consciousness
- development under constraint
- irreversibility
- non-delegable responsibility
- structural failure
- bounded projection with loss
- tension that cannot always be harmonized immediately

This is the common frame.

Everything else may vary.

---

## Hierarchy of determination

The repository should be read in this order:

1. foundation  
2. invariants  
3. models  
4. project logic  
5. style logic  
6. concrete realizations

This order matters.

- foundation defines admissible structural coherence
- invariants define pressure conditions
- models define interaction patterns
- projects define projection domains
- styles define articulation modes
- realizations instantiate local works

Lower layers may shape upper layers.
Upper layers may test lower layers.
But upper layers do not replace lower layers.

---

## Relation between project and style

A style must never float freely.

A style always belongs:

- to one project
- or to a clearly named cross-project style family

Default rule:

A style is interpreted locally within a project.

This means:

- "austere" in novel != "austere" in opera
- "romantic" in novel != "romantic" in film
- "absurd" in drama != "absurd" in essay

Terms may recur,
but their operative meaning remains project-bound
unless explicitly generalized.

---

## Cross-project style families

Where useful,
the repository may define style families
that recur across several projects.

Examples:

- austere
- ritual
- fragmentary
- polyphonic
- documentary
- romantic
- absurd
- mythic-concentrated

These are not universal styles.
They are **comparative families**.

Their function is:

- to enable transfer
- to compare reductions
- to preserve coherence across variation

A cross-project style family should define only:

- recurring structural tendency
- recurring gain
- recurring loss
- recurring tension profile

Its concrete realization must still be redefined
inside each project.

---

## Minimal admissibility test

A new project or style is admissible
when the following questions can be answered clearly:

### For projects

- What medium does it use?
- What structural focus does it select?
- What does it make visible?
- What does it systematically miss?
- Which core elements does it draw from?
- Which developmental tensions does it reveal?
- What does it explicitly not attempt?

### For styles

- How does it articulate the project?
- What does it intensify?
- What does it suppress?
- How does it treat explanation?
- What formal pressure governs it?
- Which contradictions does it sharpen?
- Why does it belong inside this project?

If these questions cannot be answered,
the projection is still too vague.

---

## Generative objective

The repository is not only meant
to store projects and styles.
It is meant to make them reusable.

The long-term objective is that
new works can be generated
from very few instructions,
because the deeper structural logic
is already defined in the repository.

This means that a prompt such as:

- "Write me a corresponding novel, but romantic."
- "Generate the same core tension as an absurd play."
- "Create a short opera version in a liturgical style."
- "Write a new book from this architecture in fragmentary prose."

should eventually be sufficient
to instantiate a new work,
as long as project and style
are already structurally specified.

In advanced form,
even a single sentence
may be enough to trigger generation,
because the sentence does not need
to carry the whole architecture.
It only needs to select:

- a project
- a style
- optionally a scope, tone, or emphasis

The rest should be recoverable
from the repository's structural frame.

---

## Minimal promptability

To support this future use case,
projects and styles should be written
in a way that makes them
**minimally promptable**.

That means:

- they should be definable in compact language
- they should expose their key operators clearly
- they should name their strengths and blind spots
- they should remain generatively reusable
- they should support instantiation
  without requiring full re-explanation each time

Good project/style definitions
should therefore function both as:

- documentation
- generation grammar

---

## Anti-patterns

The following should be avoided.

### Totalizing style claims

A style must not imply
that its own articulation mode
is the true form of the architecture.

### Unbounded projects

A project that has no named limit
cannot be structurally evaluated.

### Decorative style labeling

A style is not just an aesthetic tag.

It must describe a real reduction logic.

### Core replacement

No project or style may replace
foundation, invariants, or models
with its own local vocabulary.

### Hidden omissions

What is excluded should be nameable.

Unmarked omission produces confusion.

### Surface-only promptability

A style should not be reduced
to a decorative adjective only.

Terms like "romantic" or "absurd"
are valid only when they correspond
to a defined structural modulation.
