# Project: mysticism

**Projection space within the `life` architecture – investigation of direct encounters.**

This project examines mysticism as the domain of **direct, non-discursive, often transformative encounters** with what lies beyond ordinary perception and conceptual grasp (the transcendent, the ground of being, the absolute, or the void).

### Core Determination
Mysticism is understood here as the recurring human attempt to **experience** reality at its deepest level — not merely to believe, think or behave according to a tradition, but to encounter it immediately and ineffably (Unio mystica, samādhi, fana, satori, etc.).

### Key Aspects Investigated
- Phenomenology of mystical experiences (what is reported, what is necessarily ineffable)
- Structural features (unity, timelessness, no-self, sacred presence, dissolution of subject-object)
- Practices that facilitate such encounters (contemplation, meditation, asceticism, prayer, psychedelics)
- Apophatic vs. kataphatic approaches (via negativa / via positiva)
- Tension between the ineffable encounter and the life-invariants (especially boundedness and irreversibility)
- Relation to (and frequent conflict with) religious institutions and dogmas
- Comparative mapping across cultures and epochs

### Approach
Emphasis on honest documentation and structural analysis of already existing mystical accounts and practices. Creative projection is minimized.

### Relation to BEING, Religions and Spirituality
- **BEING** investigates the structural recurrence without redemption.  
- **Religions** map the collective systems built around that reality.  
- **Mysticism** focuses on the **rare, intense, personal breakthroughs** that transcend ordinary religious form.  
- **Spirituality** covers the broader, more everyday personal meaning-making.

Mysticism is thus the **intense experiential layer** — often inside, sometimes against religions.

### Projection Discipline
Declared reduction: careful observation and comparison. No romanticization, no new doctrine.
