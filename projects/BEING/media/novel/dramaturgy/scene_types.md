# Scene Types

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental scene types used within the dramaturgical layer.

Scenes are not primarily categorized by action or emotional tone.  
Instead, they are classified according to their **structural function within the epistemic trajectory**.

Each scene type represents a moment where structural dynamics become visible.

Scenes therefore function as **epistemic operations** rather than narrative decorations.


---

# Structural Orientation

Scenes in this framework do not primarily serve to:

- move plot forward
- increase spectacle
- create arbitrary emotional escalation

Instead they serve to:

- reveal structures
- expose contradictions
- produce interpretive shifts
- make systemic dynamics perceptible


---

# Core Scene Types

## Immersion Scenes

Function:

Introduce the protagonist into an apparently coherent system.

Characteristics:

- institutional rituals
- educational environments
- everyday routines within structured systems

Narrative role:

Establish the **local coherence** that later becomes destabilized.


---

## Fracture Scenes

Function:

Reveal the first inconsistencies within a system.

Characteristics:

- unexpected outcomes
- subtle contradictions
- unexplained anomalies

Narrative role:

Introduce **epistemic instability** without yet providing explanation.


---

## Pattern Recognition Scenes

Function:

Allow the protagonist to notice that inconsistencies repeat.

Characteristics:

- similar events across different contexts
- mirrored institutional reactions
- recurring structural problems

Narrative role:

Shift from isolated anomaly to **systemic pattern awareness**.


---

## Conflict Scenes

Function:

Expose tension between emerging insight and institutional stability.

Characteristics:

- disagreement over interpretation
- institutional defensiveness
- reframing of structural critique

Narrative role:

Make structural tensions socially visible.


---

## Insight Scenes

Function:

Produce a transformation in understanding.

Characteristics:

- reinterpretation of earlier events
- sudden conceptual clarity
- recognition of systemic dynamics

Narrative role:

Create a shift from local explanation to **structural understanding**.


---

## Isolation Scenes

Function:

Show the social consequences of structural insight.

Characteristics:

- marginalization
- withdrawal
- loss of institutional trust

Narrative role:

Demonstrate the gap between **structural knowledge and social acceptance**.


---

## Reconfiguration Scenes

Function:

Establish a new relationship to the world after insight.

Characteristics:

- new interpretive frameworks
- altered institutional relations
- quiet continuation under new awareness

Narrative role:

Create a temporary equilibrium after epistemic transformation.


---

# Relationship to the Zone Model

Scene types often correspond to transitions between epistemic zones.

Immersion → Zone A  
Fracture → Zone B  
Pattern Recognition → Zone C  
Conflict → Zone D  
Insight / Isolation / Reconfiguration → Zone E


---

# Recurrence of Scene Types

The same scene types may appear multiple times across different contexts.

Recurrence is not repetition for its own sake.  
It allows structural patterns to become visible.

For example:

- similar institutional reactions in different epochs
- repeated failures of explanation
- mirrored conflicts in different social environments


---

# Working Rule

A scene should exist only if it fulfills at least one of the following functions:

- revealing structural coherence
- exposing contradictions
- enabling pattern recognition
- triggering epistemic shifts
- demonstrating consequences of insight


---

# Summary

Scenes are the smallest dramaturgical units through which structural dynamics become perceptible.

They do not primarily advance plot.

They **expose the movement of understanding within a structured world**.
