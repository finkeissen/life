# Narrative Perspective — life

This document describes the possible development of human perspective
that may appear in projections.

It does not describe a strict rule of development,
but a spectrum of orientations through which individuals may move.

The movement often goes from self-centered perception
toward the ability to recognize and support the needs of others.

---

## Level 1 — Immediate Need

Perspective is centered on immediate personal needs.

Typical characteristics:

- survival and safety dominate perception
- little distinction between self and world
- limited ability to consider the needs of others
- strong emotional immediacy

This perspective is typical of early childhood,
but elements of it may reappear under stress or fear.

---

## Level 2 — Self-Aware Agency

The individual begins to recognize themselves as an actor
within a larger world.

Characteristics:

- awareness of consequences
- recognition of other individuals as agents
- negotiation between personal needs and external constraints
- emerging responsibility

Conflicts often arise between personal desire
and the realities of the surrounding world.

---

## Level 3 — Expanding Perspective

The individual becomes able to reflect on their own position
within broader structures.

Characteristics:

- ability to consider multiple viewpoints
- awareness of social and relational dynamics
- reflection on responsibility and long-term consequences
- increasing capacity for restraint and judgment

At this level, decisions often involve trade-offs
between different legitimate interests.

---

## Level 4 — Mature Support

Perspective expands beyond the self.

Characteristics:

- stable sense of self
- ability to support others without losing agency
- willingness to assume responsibility for shared outcomes
- long-term orientation toward collective well-being

Support for others becomes possible
not through self-negation,
but through the stability of one's own position.

---

## Narrative Function

These levels are not strict stages,
nor do they form a guaranteed progression.

Characters may move between them
depending on situation, stress, and experience.

In narrative terms, this spectrum allows exploration of:

- development
- regression
- responsibility
- conflict between maturity and self-preservation

---

## Guiding Principle

A stable ability to support others
usually emerges only after one's own basic needs
are sufficiently secured.

Attempts to bypass this often produce instability
or hidden resentment.
