# Life Timeline of Ekkehard

This document maps the epistemic development of Ekkehard across the life span.
The phases describe shifts in understanding rather than simple age categories.

---

## Phase I — Childhood / Supported Self‑Evidence
Approx. age: 0–12

Epistemic condition:
The world appears ordered, explainable, and morally stable.

Primary figures:
- Mother
- Father
- Sibling
- Primary Teacher

What is learned:
- rules precede reflection
- authority appears trustworthy
- responsibility seems delegable

Media emphasis:
- novel: high
- film: selective flashbacks
- libretto: symbolic reference only

---

## Phase II — Youth / First Irritations
Approx. age: 13–20

Epistemic condition:
Small contradictions begin to appear in the assumed order.

Primary figures:
- Friend
- Rival student
- Influential teacher

What is learned:
- rules may conflict
- institutions are fallible
- explanation does not guarantee justice

Media emphasis:
- novel: high
- film: moderate
- libretto: potential Act I material

---

## Phase III — Early Adult Life / Non‑Delegability
Approx. age: 21–35

Epistemic condition:
Responsibility becomes unavoidable.

Primary figures:
- Mentor
- Institutional authority
- Partner
- Colleague

What is learned:
- decisions produce irreversible consequences
- responsibility cannot always be transferred
- participation in structures becomes visible

Media emphasis:
- novel: high
- film: high
- libretto: Act II core

---

## Phase IV — Midlife / Loss of Moral Relief
Approx. age: 36–55

Epistemic condition:
Traditional moral explanations lose their stabilizing power.

Primary figures:
- Mirror character
- Institutional opponent
- Child / next generation

What is learned:
- innocence cannot be restored
- structures persist beyond individual intention

Media emphasis:
- novel: high
- film: high
- libretto: Act III core

---

## Phase V — Later Life / Open Continuation
Approx. age: 56+

Epistemic condition:
Recognition without resolution.

Primary figures:
- Witness
- Younger generation
- Occasional mirror figures

What is learned:
- recognition does not eliminate responsibility
- life continues without final resolution

Media emphasis:
- novel: reflective passages
- film: epilogue or frame
- libretto: closing chorus or reflective scene
