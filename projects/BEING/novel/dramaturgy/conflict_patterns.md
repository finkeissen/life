# Conflict Patterns

## Purpose

This document defines the structural conflict patterns used within the dramaturgical layer.

Conflicts in this framework do not primarily arise from personal hostility or emotional rivalry.

Instead, they emerge when **structural tensions between different layers of reality become visible**.

The purpose of conflict is therefore not to create drama for its own sake, but to reveal contradictions within systems.


---

# Nature of Structural Conflict

Traditional narratives often construct conflict around:

- opposing personal goals
- emotional rivalry
- moral confrontation

In this project, conflict arises when:

- institutional coherence encounters structural contradiction
- insight challenges systemic stability
- responsibility exceeds individual control
- explanations fail to account for observed reality

Characters do not invent these tensions.  
They encounter them.


---

# Primary Conflict Axes

## Individual vs Institution

The protagonist recognizes structural inconsistencies that institutions must ignore in order to preserve stability.

Typical manifestations:

- criticism interpreted as disloyalty
- insight interpreted as disturbance
- structural problems reframed as personal deviance


---

## Insight vs Stability

New understanding threatens the coherence of existing systems.

Institutions tend to protect stability rather than revise explanatory frameworks.

Typical manifestations:

- defensive interpretations
- reinterpretation of anomalies
- containment of disruptive knowledge


---

## Responsibility vs System Dynamics

Actors experience responsibility for outcomes produced by systems they only partially control.

This creates tension between:

- personal accountability
- systemic opacity


---

## Knowledge vs Explanation

Established explanations fail to account for emerging observations.

Actors must choose between:

- defending the existing interpretive system
- revising the explanatory framework


---

# Conflict Escalation

Structural conflicts usually escalate gradually.

Typical escalation sequence:

1. Minor inconsistency  
   A small anomaly appears but is easily rationalized.

2. Repeated anomaly  
   Similar inconsistencies appear multiple times.

3. Interpretive disagreement  
   Actors disagree about the meaning of observed events.

4. Institutional reaction  
   Structures attempt to contain interpretive disruption.

5. Structural rupture  
   The contradiction becomes impossible to ignore.


---

# Relationship to the Zone Model

Conflict patterns correspond to transitions between epistemic zones.

Zone A → Zone B  
First inconsistencies appear.

Zone B → Zone C  
Patterns of contradiction emerge.

Zone C → Zone D  
Institutional resistance intensifies.

Zone D → Zone E  
Structural insight becomes unavoidable.


---

# Conflict and Ensemble Structures

Different ensemble configurations often express different forms of conflict.

Examples:

Individual perspective  
→ internal interpretive tension

Dialogic encounter  
→ competing interpretations

Institutional ensemble  
→ defense of systemic coherence

Structural chorus  
→ articulation of systemic dynamics


---

# Recurrence of Conflict

Structural conflicts tend to recur across:

- different institutions
- different historical contexts
- different social roles

These recurrences reveal that the conflict is not accidental but structural.


---

# Working Rule

A conflict is structurally valid if it reveals at least one of the following:

- systemic contradiction
- limits of institutional coherence
- tension between insight and stability
- recurring patterns of structural blindness


---

# Summary

Conflict in this framework is not primarily about victory or defeat.

Its function is to reveal **structural contradictions within locally coherent orders**.
