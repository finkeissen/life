# Narrative Topology

## Purpose

This document describes the structural topology of the narrative system.

Narratives in this project are not organized as simple linear sequences.

Instead, they form a multi-layered structure in which:

- different epochs
- different protagonists
- different epistemic arcs

interact and overlap.

The narrative topology defines how these elements are arranged within the overall architecture.


---

# Linear vs Structural Narrative

Traditional narrative topology is linear.

Example:

beginning → development → climax → resolution

In this project, narrative organization is primarily structural rather than chronological.

Events are arranged so that structural patterns become visible across time and context.


---

# Topological Dimensions

The narrative system operates across several dimensions simultaneously.

## Temporal Dimension

Different historical periods may appear within the same narrative.

Examples:

- monastic epoch
- theological epoch
- modern institutional epoch

These periods do not necessarily appear in chronological order.


---

## Epistemic Dimension

Narrative movement follows changes in interpretation rather than simple sequences of events.

Example progression:

immersion → fracture → pattern recognition → structural insight


---

## Structural Dimension

Events in different contexts may express the same structural tension.

Examples:

- institutional resistance to critique
- repeated explanatory failure
- recurring conflicts between stability and insight


---

# Topological Structures

Several structural forms may appear within the narrative topology.

## Parallel Structures

Different narrative strands unfold simultaneously.

Each strand may represent a different epoch or protagonist.


---

## Mirror Structures

Events in different contexts reveal the same structural dynamics.

These mirrored events allow recognition of recurring patterns.


---

## Nested Structures

Smaller epistemic arcs may occur within larger arcs.

Example:

scene arc → chapter arc → narrative arc → cross-epoch arc


---

## Recurrence Structures

Certain tensions and situations reappear across narrative layers.

This recurrence reveals structural invariants.


---

# Topological Movement

Narrative movement may occur along different axes:

temporal movement  
→ shift between historical epochs

epistemic movement  
→ change in interpretive framework

structural movement  
→ recognition of systemic patterns


---

# Role of the Protagonist

Protagonists are not always the primary drivers of narrative topology.

Instead, they function as observers and interpreters moving through structural configurations.


---

# Relationship to Dramaturgy

The dramaturgy layer defines how individual narrative elements operate.

The narrative topology defines how these elements are arranged across the entire narrative system.

Together they determine:

- where scenes appear
- how arcs interact
- how structural patterns become visible.


---

# Working Rule

Narrative structure should prioritize structural clarity over chronological continuity.

Events should be arranged so that structural relationships become perceptible.


---

# Summary

The narrative topology organizes the spatial structure of the narrative system.

It allows multiple epochs, arcs, and structural patterns to interact within a single narrative framework.
