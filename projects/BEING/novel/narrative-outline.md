# Narrative Outline (Novel Projection)

This document outlines how the BEING architecture unfolds in the novel format.
It follows the life timeline but allows deeper psychological and relational exploration.

## Part I — Childhood (Supported Self-Evidence)
Focus:
- Early family environment
- Formation of trust in authority and explanation
- First stable interpretation of the world

Key figures:
- Mother
- Father
- Sibling
- Primary teacher

Narrative function:
Establish the moral and interpretive framework that later becomes unstable.

---

## Part II — Youth (First Irritations)

Focus:
- First contradictions within institutions and rules
- Intellectual awakening and social tension

Key figures:
- Friend
- Rival student
- Influential teacher

Narrative function:
Introduce structural doubt without immediate collapse of meaning.

---

## Part III — Early Adulthood (Non‑Delegable Responsibility)

Focus:
- Professional life begins
- Institutional participation
- First irreversible decisions

Key figures:
- Mentor
- Institutional authority
- Partner
- Colleagues

Narrative function:
Responsibility becomes personal and unavoidable.

---

## Part IV — Midlife (Loss of Moral Relief)

Focus:
- Recognition of structural consequences
- Moral narratives fail to stabilize experience

Key figures:
- Mirror character
- Institutional opponent
- Child / next generation

Narrative function:
Collapse of the belief that innocence can be restored.

---

## Part V — Later Life (Open Continuation)

Focus:
- Reflection without final resolution
- Structural recognition persists

Key figures:
- Witness
- Younger generation

Narrative function:
Recognition replaces narrative closure.
