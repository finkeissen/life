# Models — life

Models organize how invariants interact within concrete situations.

While invariants describe structural conditions,
models describe patterns through which those conditions become visible.

Models are not fixed.
They may evolve when projections reveal blind spots
or structural weaknesses.

---

## Decision Situations

A decision situation occurs when an actor must choose
between multiple possible actions under constraint.

Characteristics:

- incomplete knowledge
- limited time
- conflicting values
- uncertain outcomes

Decisions in such situations reshape future possibilities.

---

## Responsibility Structures

Responsibility emerges from the relationship
between action, consequence, and agency.

A responsibility structure includes:

- the actor
- the decision context
- affected others
- the distribution of authority
- the visibility of consequences

Responsibility often becomes most visible
when systems attempt to diffuse it.

---

## Development Dynamics

Development describes how individuals or groups
change over time through interaction with constraints.

Development may involve:

- learning
- moral conflict
- adaptation
- failure
- transformation of goals or identity

Development is rarely linear.

It often includes regressions, disruptions,
and unexpected turning points.

---

## Conflict Structures

Conflict arises when:

- goals collide
- resources are limited
- values differ
- or interpretations of reality diverge

Conflicts reveal underlying structures
that remain invisible in stable situations.

They are therefore critical testing grounds
for the framework.

---

## Role of Models

Models help translate abstract invariants
into analyzable situations.

They are tools for thinking and designing projections,
not fixed representations of reality.
